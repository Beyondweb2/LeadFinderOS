CHOOSING YOUR CAR

Manual or automatic?

If you’re not sure, think about the car you expect to drive and what you’d like help with during lessons.

Learning in a manual

You’ll learn to use the clutch and change gears as well as steering, observing and judging traffic. It can feel like a lot to coordinate at first. Those controls are part of what you practise.

A manual is worth considering if that’s the type of car you plan to use after learning.

Explore manual lessons →

Learning in an automatic

With no clutch pedal or manual gear changes, there are fewer controls to coordinate. You’ll still work on the decisions that matter on the road: when to move, where to look and how much space to leave.

Ask about automatic lessons if you’d prefer to learn without managing gears.

BEFORE YOU START

Your first lesson starts with a conversation

It’s fine to say you’re nervous. It also helps to say what’s worrying you—stalling, other drivers, busy junctions or simply not knowing what to expect.

Talk about what you’ve done

If you’ve had lessons before, mention when you last drove, which car you learned in and what still needed work. If you’re brand new, just say so.

Sort out the practical details

Before the lesson, agree the meeting point, length and price. Ask what you need to bring, and make sure you know how to contact your instructor if your plans change.

Leave with something to work on

At the end of a lesson, ask what went well and what needs more practice. A short note on your phone can help you remember the feedback next time.

WHAT YOU’LL PRACTISE

Think beyond the test route

The skills you’re learning are the ones you’ll use when you’re driving to work, visiting friends or finding a space in a busy car park.

Junctions and roundabouts

Reading the road, choosing your position and deciding when there’s a safe gap.

Parking and slow control

Keeping the car under control while checking the space and people around you.

Driving independently

Following directions, planning ahead and making decisions without relying on prompts.
